# CEO Review: Espresso Finder Japan (PRD v0.1)

**Verdict: Don't fund.** For now this is a hobby project with a pitch-deck paragraph attached. I'll look again after M0 if it passes the kill criteria the PRD sets for itself.

---

## Top 3 killer objections

### 1. The only evidence you have argues against building this
> "It wasn't a big problem, so I didn't try." / "I just drank any coffee"

The PRD has one interview, and that interviewee says the problem doesn't matter. The document admits it: "The problem is not yet validated for the core segment." Everything else in the problem table ("3–5 times a week", "at least 30 minutes") describes the founder, written in first person ("I want freshly brewed espresso coffee"). That's one person's itch dressed up as a market. Section 10 says to stop if interviewees say "it is not a big problem (as the first interviewee did)". So by the founder's own rule, the evidence so far points to stopping. I won't fund a bet that its only data point has already voted against.

### 2. No market size, and the segment shrinks every time you define it
The document never gives a number. The primary user is a foreign resident who "can tell a semi-automatic machine shot from fully automatic or drip" and "will walk 10–15 minutes further". That cuts a small population (foreign residents in Japan) down to coffee obsessives within it, then down to one ward: "one ward / around one university", "50–100 cafes". Section 12 asks "Is the core segment large enough, and reachable?" That isn't a risk to note at the end. It's the question you have to answer before asking for money. The country list ("Italy, France, Australia, Korea, the UK, China") reads as a guess, not data. Nothing here shows a venture-scale ceiling, or even a lifestyle-business floor.

### 3. The moat is a spreadsheet one person keeps up, and incumbents can absorb it
> "a verified dataset of *how* cafes in Japan make coffee — a fact that existing map and review platforms do not record in a structured way."

A one-founder curation of 50–100 cafes is not a compounding asset. Anyone with a weekend and Instagram can copy it. The PRD rules out the one mechanism that could make it compound: "Users adding cafes" is pushed to *Later*. Freshness depends on the founder: "re-verify each cafe every 6 months". The PRD also names the risk that kills it, "Will Google Maps / Tabelog add a 'coffee type' attribute?", and gives no answer. Google already has structured place attributes and crowdsourced Q&A. If this ever looks valuable, it becomes a checkbox in their product. The product also sends users to the incumbent on purpose ("hands off to Google Maps"), so you're building the funnel for the platform you'd compete with.

---

## Other issues

- **No "why now."** Nothing has changed in the market, technology, tourism policy, or competition that makes 2026 the moment. Espresso machines and foreign residents both existed in 2016.
- **No "why this founder."** "Owner: founder (solo)" is the only credential. There's no coffee-industry network, no audience, no distribution, and nothing that explains how one person scales verification beyond a single ward (the PRD's own open question: "Can one person keep data accurate as the area grows?").
- **The business model is a wish list.** "Featured placement", "Partnerships with roasters", "Licensing the verified coffee dataset". None has a buyer, a price, or a single conversation behind it. Paid placement also undermines the "verified" neutrality the company claims as its asset.
- **The kill criteria are too lax.** "Nobody opens the page for one month" is a bar almost nothing fails; one friend's visit passes it. Meanwhile the success metric "≥ 5 of 8" interviews depends entirely on recruiting from X/Threads, which will select for people who already agree.
- **The vision doesn't match the scope.** "A small, data-first company" whose product is a free single-ward finder that, by design, is "not a review site, not an ordering app". There's no path from v1 to a company.
- **The secondary segment contradicts the product.** Tourists ("visiting coffee tourist") need coverage of many cities. v1 covers one ward.
- **The pain is overstated.** Going from "~30 minutes" to "under 5 minutes" assumes 30 minutes is typical. The only interviewee spent zero.

---

## What would change my mind

1. **Finish M0 and show me the transcripts.** At least 8 espresso-focused interviewees, recruited outside the founder's own network, with 5+ rating the pain 4/5 or higher, plus evidence of current workarounds (lists, group chats, paid guides).
2. **A bottom-up market number.** Count the foreign residents and visitors in the launch city who fit the persona, show how you'll reach them, and show what one acquired user costs.
3. **A demand test before building anything.** A Google Sheet or Notion list of 30 cafes posted to two communities, with real return-visit and share numbers.
4. **A credible answer on defensibility.** Either a contribution model (cafes or users verify themselves) that scales without the founder, or a reason Google won't simply add the attribute.
5. **One letter of intent.** From a roaster, equipment brand, or tourism/relocation business that says it would pay for the dataset or placement, with a figure.
6. **Tighter kill criteria.** For example, fewer than 200 weekly users, or under 20% week-2 retention, at day 60 means stop.
