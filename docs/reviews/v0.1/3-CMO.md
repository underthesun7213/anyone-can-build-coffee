# CMO Review: Espresso Finder Japan (PRD v0.1)

## Verdict
This is a dataset with no distribution plan behind it. The plan targets a niche the PRD cannot yet show exists, and it asks for a weekly habit from a product that people need once.

## Top 3 killer objections

**1. The "first 1,000 users" plan is a single line.** All of acquisition is M2: *"Public v1 link; share in foreign-resident and coffee communities."* There are no named communities, no seeding cadence, no content angle and no budget. Nobody owns it beyond a founder who is also verifying 50–100 cafes *"by visiting... and calling where needed."* The product has *"no install, no sign-up"* and *"No accounts in v1,"* so there is no email, no push and no saved list (that is deferred to *Later*). Every visitor you buy or earn is anonymous and leaves at once. You cannot re-engage them, so you cannot compound them.

**2. The retention target conflicts with the product's own success moment.** The success moment is *"user leaves the page with a destination within 5 minutes,"* followed by *"Tap 'Directions' → hands off to Google Maps."* The persona is an *"espresso regular"* in *one ward*. Once that person finds their two good cafes, the job is done and they never come back. Yet the PRD targets *"≥ 30% of first-week visitors return in week 2."* That is a strong benchmark even for apps that have accounts and notifications. Here there are no favorites, no "new cafe added" alerts and no reason to reopen. The *"3–5 times a week"* frequency describes drinking coffee. It does not describe searching for coffee. Repeat use falls to roughly zero once the user's own list is built, and the PRD itself says that list already exists (*"keep a personal list of cafes"*).

**3. Demand is unproven, and the one data point argues against it.** *"Evidence so far. One interview... 'I just drank any coffee'; 'It wasn't a big problem, so I didn't try.'"* The PRD admits *"The problem is not yet validated."* The positioning still assumes a segment that *"can tell a semi-automatic machine shot from fully automatic."* That is an enthusiast slice of a minority (foreign residents) inside one ward. Do the arithmetic: one ward × foreign residents × daily espresso drinkers × people who care about the machine type. That could be a few hundred people. G3, *"Prove demand in one area,"* may succeed technically and still prove nothing about scale.

## Other issues

- **Naming.** "Espresso Finder Japan (working name)" is a description, not a brand. It also excludes the "Fresh drip" and siphon users your own filters serve. It is hard to remember, hard to share and generic for SEO.
- **Positioning sentence.** *"instead of searching for 30 minutes like a detective"*: the 30 minutes comes from the founder's own experience, and the only interviewee contradicts it. Lead with the outcome people want, such as "real espresso, not a button machine," rather than time saved.
- **Competition for attention.** The PRD frames competition as features (*"Will Google Maps / Tabelog add a 'coffee type' attribute?"*). The real threat is habit. Your users already open Google Maps and Instagram. Your own flow sends them back to Google Maps, so you train them to stay there. Instagram coffee accounts, specialty-coffee map lists such as saved Google lists, and Reddit r/JapanTravel threads already answer "where is good espresso near X" for free and come with social proof. The PRD never names them.
- **No virality mechanic.** You can't share a list, there's no shareable card, no "I found it here" moment and no badge a cafe would display. Cafes are your best free distribution channel. A "Verified Espresso" window sticker or an Instagram story tag would turn 50 cafes into 50 billboards, and the PRD ignores them.
- **Kill criterion is too weak.** *"Nobody opens the page for one month"* will never trigger, because the founder's friends will open it. Set a number, for example fewer than 300 unique visitors or fewer than 50 returning visitors in 8 weeks.
- **Metrics with no funnel.** Nothing measures acquisition: no visitor target, no channel attribution, no cost per user. *"Directions click-through ≥ 40%"* measures handing users off to a competitor, and *"Wrong-info reports < 5%"* is a quality metric, not a growth metric.
- **The tourist segment is the bigger market, and you deprioritize it.** Visitors search before their trip (on Google, TikTok and blogs), which is a channel you can actually reach with SEO. Residents are not searching.

## A concrete go-to-market fix

1. **Lead with SEO and content, not a map.** Publish static, indexable pages such as "Real espresso near Shibuya Station" and "Flat white in Kyoto: verified machines," one per station. Tourists search for exactly this before they travel. The verified dataset becomes the content moat.
2. **Use cafes as a channel.** Give each verified cafe a QR sticker ("Verified espresso · see the menu") and a ready-made Instagram post. Ask each one to repost. 50 cafes × their followers is your first 1,000 users.
3. **Seed 3 named communities with posts that add value.** Pick specific Reddit subs, Facebook expat groups and university international offices. Post a free "espresso map of [ward]" image, not a link drop.
4. **Add a minimal retention hook before launch.** Offer an optional email or LINE signup for "new verified cafes near you" plus a monthly "new openings" digest. Without it, the week-2 retention target is fiction.
5. **Rename around a promise you can own**, for example "RealShot" or "Machine Check," and test 3 names with the 8 M0 interviewees.
6. **Replace the metrics with a funnel:** unique visitors per channel, signups per visitor, sources of repeat visits, and cafe reposts. Set a numeric kill gate at week 8.
