# CMO review — PRD v0.5 (Coffee brewing finder, Japan)

## Verdict
The espresso pivot leaves the product with a hook that, in this launch area, answers "yes" almost every time. The channel plan has no search, no owned audience and only 30 cafes to post about. As written, it won't get past novelty traffic.

## Top 3 killer objections

**1. The headline question has a near-obvious answer in Shibuya/Omotesando/Harajuku.**
§1: "It answers one question first: *does this cafe make espresso on a machine, and what kind?*" In the three most specialty-dense stations in Tokyo, nearly every third-wave cafe and nearly every chain (Starbucks, Tully's, Doutor, Excelsior) has an espresso machine. The uncertainty is not "is there espresso" but "semi vs. fully automatic", and the filter labels that "Hand-made espresso only". That distinction matters to espresso regulars and means nothing to the "cafe hoppers" and "visitors" in §3. M0 also doesn't test this hook. The uncertainty test counts cafes with "a brewing method you cannot find on Google", and the list test shares "brewing methods". The ≥20% gate could pass on siphon and hand-drip ambiguity while the new espresso positioning stays unvalidated. Meanwhile the one non-founder interview "said it 'wasn't a big problem.'" The pivot narrowed the audience to the founder's own habit and gave no new evidence for it.

**2. The growth plan cuts the one channel that fits the stated users, then depends on content it can't keep up.**
§3 names "visitors planning coffee stops". Those people search Google ("espresso Harajuku"). But §4 lists "station pages for search" as a non-goal, and §16 repeats "Not in v1: … station pages for search." That leaves Reddit and X. The doc says "short weekly posts on X", each linking a filtered view. With 25–30 cafes in 3 stations, the distinct filtered views worth posting run out in about two months. The doc's own example post, "Cafes near Omotesando that brew by siphon", is off-positioning: it promotes a secondary method. A new X account with no followers gets close to zero reach. Reddit (r/JapanTravel, r/Tokyo) bans or removes self-promotion from new accounts, and the doc has no plan for that. All of this comes out of the same 8 hours that also cover research, corrections and redeploys (§11, F5).

**3. There's no reason to come back, and no metric that would show it.**
A user who learns which 4–5 Omotesando cafes pull shots on a semi-automatic machine has no reason to return. The list is static and grows only at the founder's pace. Visitors use it once per trip. "Direct visits" is the only retention signal, and its target is "set after month 1", so no retention goal exists before launch. The traffic target "≥ 100 weekly visits by month 3" can be hit by a single Reddit post that week and missed the next. Visits per week can't tell a spike from a habit, so the §15 stop rule will be decided by luck of timing.

## Other issues
- **No name, and the working name contradicts the positioning.** "Coffee brewing finder" says "brewing", while the product now says espresso. M0 step 4 tests 3 names, but they aren't listed, and "fixed before anything public" collides with the M0 list test, which *is* public.
- **The chain guide competes with free answers.** "Which Japanese coffee chains make espresso on a machine?" is answered by each chain's menu page and a dozen travel blogs. Every entry also carries "May differ by store", which weakens the one thing the product sells: sourced certainty.
- **The Japanese-audience rationale is a guess.** "Many Japanese coffee people are used to dark-roast drip; the espresso filter lets them find something different" has no source, and no interviewee was asked about it. Most dark-roast kissaten fans are not looking for espresso.
- **Competition is underspecified.** §15 names only Google Maps and Tabelog. Instagram coffee curators, European Coffee Trip, Beanhunter and existing "Tokyo specialty coffee" maps already hold this audience's attention, and the doc never says why someone would switch.
- **Word of mouth is weak.** F7 share lists have no title, no author and, as far as the doc says, no link-preview image (it only specifies page titles and descriptions). A pasted link in LINE or X shows nothing worth tapping.
- **The M0 list test community is unnamed.** "Don't recruit in the planned launch communities" can't be checked because no communities are named anywhere.
- **"≥ 5 unprompted requests" is undefined.** Requests for what, through which channel?

## A concrete go-to-market fix
1. **Re-run M0 against the actual hook.** Make the uncertainty test count "semi vs. fully automatic unclear on Google or Ask Maps in 2 minutes". If that rate is under 20%, the espresso positioning fails and should be dropped before build.
2. **Pick one audience: English-speaking specialty visitors.** They have the clearest one-time, high-intent need, and a non-Japanese founder-built site has an edge with them. Name the product for that need, for example an "espresso map of Tokyo" style name.
3. **Allow 3 static station pages for search.** One per launch station, each titled "Espresso in {station}: semi-automatic machines, sourced". They cost little and are the only channel that keeps working when the founder isn't posting.
4. **Replace weekly X posts with one launch post in each of 2 named communities** (say, one English subreddit and one Japanese coffee community). Get moderator clearance first. Then spend the saved hours adding stations, because new stations are the only thing that creates new reasons to visit.
5. **Add a link-preview image to shared lists and cafe pages**, and give shared lists an editable title.
6. **Set a retention target before launch**: for example, direct visits ≥ 15% of weekly visits by month 3. Judge the §15 floor on a 4-week average, not on one week.
